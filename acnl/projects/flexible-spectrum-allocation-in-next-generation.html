The project

Spectrum allocation algorithms for elastic optical networks, the line of work that led to the FUSION simulator.

PI Vinod Vokkarane.

Award number: 2008530

Students

Arash Rezaee, Ryan McCann

From the authors of the papers acknowledging this award.

Architecture

What the work found

  • Experimental Determination of Filter Bandwidth Requirements for Coherent Pluggable Transceivers in Optical Data Center Networks (2026): Measured bandwidth requirements for coherent pluggables that flexible-grid ROADM planning can use directly, rather than rules of thumb.
  • FUSION: A Unified Benchmarking Framework for Reproducible Optical Network Research (2026): Case studies show the framework benchmarking heterogeneous architectures and policies on equal terms, and a mapping of published studies onto FUSION's capabilities shows how much of the field's recent work it can already reproduce.
  • QoT-Aware Dynamic Resource Allocation and Grooming in Multi-Band Space-Division Multiplexing Networks (2026): Ignoring frequency-dependent crosstalk causes systematic, band-dependent errors in which modulation formats are feasible in dense-core fibers. With few cores, multi-core fiber blocks less traffic than fiber bundles; with many cores, crosstalk dominates and bundles win. For seven cores, grooming pushes the onset of blocking to a 77.7 percent higher load, and the band-slot-core policy with grooming blocks 44.6 percent less than the bundle baseline across loads of 1,837 to 3,675 Tbps.
  • QoT-Aware Spectral and Spatial Scaling Trade-offs in Multi-Band Space Division Multiplexing over EONs [Invited] (2026): The best band plan depends on the core count. C+L wins for 4 and 7 cores; C+S wins for 13 cores because the L-band is hit harder by crosstalk; at 19 cores the system is crosstalk-dominated and only C+S holds up. Adding cores can shrink usable capacity: with C+L+S, maximum throughput falls from 107.94 Pbps at 13 cores to 44.76 Pbps at 19 on the U.S. backbone topology.
